Rules cheat sheet Shooters Pool League Rules & Bylaws, 2026 revision · CSI/BCA rules apply where these are silent

Instant loss of game

  • Foul (incl. scratch) while pocketing the 8
  • 8 in the wrong or uncalled pocket
  • 8 and your last ball on the same stroke
  • 8 pocketed when it isn't your legal object ball
  • 8 jumped off the table
  • 8 on the break with a scratch (10-0)

Scoring

Each object ball = 1 pt, the 8-ball = 3 pts, so a game is worth 10. That's where the points on this page come from. 9 games a night: 3 players, each plays 3.

Handicap

Add the 3 shooters' skill ratings per team. The lower team gets the difference added to its final match total. It never touches individual game scores.

Skill rating

Total points ÷ games played. You get an official rating after 3 matches (9 games). Forfeits are 10-0 but don't count as games played.

Lineup

Visiting captain posts the first shooter, home captain the next, then alternate. Score sheet shows who breaks. Home team keeps the official sheet.

Racking

Non-breaking team racks: 8 in the center, first ball on the foot spot, a stripe in one back corner and a solid in the other. Breaker may inspect the rack.

Roster

3 to 6 players; only 3 shoot per night. Adds allowed through the end of the first half only, and the Statistician must approve before the new player shoots. An ineligible player forfeits their games.

Playoffs

Played as a regular match, highest score wins. You need 16 games (4 league nights) to be eligible.

Money

$5 per shooter per night in the team envelope ($15/night). Forfeited games still owe. Payouts at the mid-season and end-of-season parties.

Shooting the 8: what ends the game and what doesn't

You're on the 8 and…Result
You scratch, 8 stays on the tableFoul · ball in hand, game continues
You miss the 8 and nothing hits a railFoul · ball in hand, game continues
Cue ball hits an opponent's ball first, 8 doesn't dropFoul · ball in hand, game continues
You scratch and the 8 dropsLoss
8 goes in the wrong or uncalled pocketLoss
8 and your last group ball drop on the same strokeLoss
8 jumps off the tableLoss
8 drops while you still have group balls upLoss · accident or not

The 8 has to actually be pocketed or jumped for the game to end (Rule 19). The real risk of a foul on the 8 is handing your opponent ball in hand, not losing on the spot.

Safeties (Rule 12)

  1. Say it first. Tell your opponent "safety" before the shot. A safety you didn't announce isn't a safety.
  2. It still has to be a legal shot. Hit your own ball first, then pocket a ball or get the cue ball or any numbered ball to a rail. Otherwise it's a foul and ball in hand.
  3. Your turn ends after the shot, even if you pocket your ball.
  4. Anything you pocket stays down. A ball pocketed on a declared safety counts as illegally pocketed, so it doesn't earn you another shot, but it doesn't come back up either.
  5. Forgot to call it? If you pocket one of your balls, you must keep shooting. You can't bail out of your turn after the fact.
  6. You don't need to declare a safety on a shot that isn't going to pocket anything. Just play the leave; if you make a legal hit, your turn ends normally.

Why it matters: a called safety lets you dump an easy ball to leave your opponent nothing, without being forced to keep shooting from a bad position.

How to rack (Rule 3)

  1. Non-breaking team racks. Breaker may inspect before breaking.
  2. Apex ball on the foot spot. Any ball works; the 1 is customary.
  3. 8-ball in the center (middle of the third row).
  4. Back corners: one stripe, one solid. Which side is which doesn't matter.
  5. Everything else is random. Mixing solids and stripes is normal. The layout shown is one legal example.
  6. Rack tight. Balls touching, apex frozen to the row behind it.

Breaking (Rules 4, 6–8)

  1. Score sheet says who breaks. Cue ball anywhere behind the head string.
  2. Legal break: pocket a ball, or send at least 4 object balls to a rail. Otherwise it's a foul and the opponent can take the table or re-rack and choose the breaker.
  3. Scratch on the break: foul, balls stay down, table stays open, opponent gets ball in hand behind the head string.
  4. 8 on the break: win, 10-7. With a scratch: loss, 10-0.
  5. Table is open after every break, no matter what drops.
What happens if I scratch while shooting the 8-ball?

If the 8-ball doesn't go in (and isn't jumped off the table), it's just a foul: your turn ends and your opponent gets ball in hand. If you pocket the 8 and scratch on the same shot, that's a loss of game. (Rules 19, 23)

I sank the 8 by accident. Is it an automatic loss?

Yes. If the 8 goes in when it isn't your legal object ball (you still have balls of your group up), it's a loss of game, accident or not. There's no spotting the 8 and playing on in this league. The only exception is the break: 8 on the break is a win (10-7), or a loss if you also scratch (10-0). (Rules 8, 17, 23e)

What counts as ball in hand?

After any foul (except where the break rules say otherwise) the incoming player may place the cue ball anywhere on the table, by hand or with the cue. Any forward stroke that touches the cue ball starts the shot. There's no minimum distance the cue ball has to travel. (Rule 15)

I scratched on the break. Now what?

It's a foul. Any balls made stay down, the table stays open, and your opponent gets ball in hand behind the head string (not anywhere). (Rule 7)

What if the 8-ball goes in on the break?

Clean break (no scratch): breaker wins, scored 10-7. Break with a scratch: breaker loses, scored 10-0. (Rules 7, 8)

What's a legal break?

Cue ball behind the head string, then either pocket a ball or drive at least 4 numbered balls to a rail. Fail that and it's a foul: your opponent can take the table as it lies, or re-rack and choose who breaks. (Rule 6)

I made a stripe on the break. Am I stripes?

No. The table is always open after the break. Groups are set only when someone legally pockets a called ball after the break. (Rule 10)

Can I hit the 8-ball first on an open table?

No, that's a foul. On an open table you can use the 8 in a combination, but the cue ball can't touch it first. (Rules 9, 16)

Do I have to call every shot?

Obvious shots don't need calling. Banks, combos and anything non-obvious do: name the ball and the pocket (not the rails or kisses). The 8-ball pocket must always be clearly called; 8 in the wrong or uncalled pocket is a loss. (Rule 2)

What's a legal shot once groups are set?

Hit one of your own balls first, then either pocket a numbered ball or send the cue ball or any numbered ball to a rail. (Rule 11)

Can I play safe by pocketing my own ball?

Yes, but you must say "safety" before the shot. If you don't, and you pocket your ball, you have to keep shooting. The ball stays down either way. (Rule 12)

I accidentally pocketed an opponent's ball. Does it come back up?

No. Illegally pocketed balls stay pocketed, and your turn ends if it was a foul. An illegally pocketed 8 is a loss. (Rule 17)

A ball flew off the table.

Foul and loss of turn; the ball is spotted. If it's the 8-ball, that's a loss of game. (Rule 18)

I made my last ball and the 8 on the same stroke.

Loss of game. (Rule 23b)

Can I jump or masse around a ball?

You can try, but if the blocking ball moves (by hand, cue, bridge, or follow-through) it's a cue ball foul. (Rule 5)

Does three fouls in a row lose the game?

No. The three-consecutive-fouls rule doesn't apply in this league. (Rule 15)

How many timeouts do I get?

2 coaching timeouts per game, 2 minutes each, one coach only. The coach can talk and point but can't touch balls or take practice strokes. Going over time after a warning is a foul. (Rule 20)

Is there a shot clock?

Only if a captain asks for it. Once announced, it's 1 minute per shot and running over is a foul. (Rule 21)

My opponent is 10 minutes late for our game.

They get a 15-minute grace period from when the game is called. After that it's a 10-0 forfeit (recorded FW/FL) that doesn't count toward anyone's games played or rating. They can still play later games. (Rule 22)

Someone bumped a ball.

Stop play; captains (or an agreed neutral) restore the ball as closely as possible. If it changed the shot, touched the cue ball, or is otherwise a foul under CSI rules, the foul penalty applies. Outside interference (not a player) is restored with no penalty. (Rules 24, 27)

Nobody can make progress on the table.

If both players or the captains agree it's a stalemate, re-rack and replay. The original breaker breaks again. (Rule 26)

We disagree on a rule mid-game.

Stop play. Captains try to settle it using these rules and CSI rules. If they can't, finish the game under protest and write the facts on the score sheet for the Board. Infractions must be raised before the next shot. (Rules 23, 28)
